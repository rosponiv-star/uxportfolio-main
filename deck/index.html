Smart Home Hub

An Android app that brings every device of a smart home into one place, designed to settle doubts rather than add controls.

Type
Product and UX design
Year
2025
Role
End-to-end UX designer
Team
3 design students

Overview

For households sharing a smart home: see the whole house, let people in safely, stop wondering.

Problem

More devices brought more doubts: did I leave it on, who rang, who can get in while I’m out?

Approach

We modelled the house as a system, mapped 16 pain points on 6 personas and designed for those that weighed most.

Outcome

An Android app in Material Design 3: floor plan, intercom, temporary access codes, security. Safety first.

Course

Graphical Interface Design (PIG) · University of Trento

Timeline

Sep – Dec 2025

My role

UX/UI designer in a team of 3. Every phase shared; I led the domain modelling, the design space, the UX flows from low to high fidelity, the intercom and its slide-to-open, and the access codes.

Methods

Domain models, proto-personas, design space, UX flows

Tools

Figma, FigJam, Material Design 3

A smart home is shared: family, babysitters, couriers and guests all pass through it.

The brief: software for a company that makes, sells, installs and services home-automation hardware. The app manages the devices already in the house, so customers stay and come back for the next one.

In scope

Every installed device: lights, appliances, alarm, sensors, cameras, intercom, gate and door.

Out of scope, by brief

Selling, installing and supporting the hardware.

Three themes behind the sixteen pain points, and the direction they set.

What they have in common

  1. 01Doubt, not comfort
    The technology made people unsure: did I really turn off the light, arm the alarm?
  2. 02Trust is unsolved
    Keys and shared codes don’t fit babysitters, couriers and guests.
  3. 03Fear of the wrong button
    Older and less expert users fear a tap that can’t be undone.

Design direction

A hub for security and management, not a universal remote.

  1. 01Everything in one view
    A dashboard of the whole home, appliances or security.
  2. 02Dynamic access
    Who gets in, and until when, instead of shared keys and codes.
  3. 03Safety first
    Sliders instead of switches for critical actions, and confirmations: usable in a hurry or with motor difficulties.

How might we let a household see, open and protect its home without adding doubt?

Design principles

  1. 01The state always in sight
    From “doubt, not comfort”. A floor plan and notifications; rules out settings hidden in menus.
  2. 02Let people in without keys
    From “trust is unsolved”. Personal, temporary, revocable codes; rules out one shared PIN.
  3. 03Safe even in a hurry
    From “fear of the wrong button”. Deliberate gestures for what can’t be undone; rules out one-tap toggles for door and alarm.

02

Individual design concepts

Each of us alone, pencil on paper, for each pain point × persona: different personas need different solutions to the same pain point. Every idea was welcome, even very creative ones: several solutions each, not judged yet. In Converge they were weighed by how well they solved the problem and how feasible they were.

03

Design space

All the concepts on one board: we aligned as a team, grouped them by affinity across personas and picked the best, checking that a solution to one pain point did not get in another persona’s way.

Under each persona, a user story in one fixed format: “As …, I want …, so that …”: who, the need, the reason.

04

Happiness factors

On each board the solutions are split into primary and secondary, then stamped solved or not. Primary ones had to be solved: they became the happiness factors, again per persona. Secondary ones were welcome, or got solved indirectly, by another pain point or by the primary solutions.

Who gets in, through what, and who decides: the app as the household’s gatekeeper.

Opening the gate from anywhere: a slide, not a tap.

  1. 01Trigger
    The intercom rings while you are out: a notification says which bell rang.
  2. 02Action
    Answer, see who it is, and slide to open the gate: one continuous, deliberate gesture.
  3. 03Feedback
    The gate opens; every call, answered or missed, stays in the history.
  4. 04Why it works
    Opening the house can’t be undone. A slide can’t happen by mistake, and no dialog covers the live video: knowingly off the MD3 guideline.

Design principles

  1. 01Fit fifteen to twenty minutes
    From “time filters everything”. Rules out recipes that need planning ahead.
  2. 02Sized for one, planned for the real week
    From waste and broken plans. Rules out family packs and fixed daily menus.
  3. 03Fewer decisions, not more knowledge
    From “effort, not ignorance”. Rules out another nutrition-education app.

Human–Computer Interaction 1 · University of Trento

AuraWake

An alarm with no buttons that wakes you gradually, with light, sound, scent and daylight, at the lightest moment of your sleep.

Type
Interaction and Integrated product design
Year
2024 · detailed in 2026
Role
Interaction and Product Designer
Team
2 design students

Problem and project

Better to wake a little early from light sleep than on time from deep sleep.

The brief

An alarm for people who struggle to wake up and feel charged in the morning, and often hit snooze. A fixed alarm often finds them in deep sleep: groggy and slow, often for half an hour.

Why a little early

In N1, N2 and REM the brain runs faster waves, close to waking. Woken there, even minutes before the set time, you are alert sooner than on time from deep sleep.

The project

AuraWake: a bracelet reads heart rate and movement and tells a base with no buttons when sleep turns light. The room wakes you then, inside a window of 30 minutes that ends at the set time. The phone only sets the plan.

Schematic night, after the AASM sleep stages. Sleep inertia: Tassi & Muzet, Sleep Medicine Reviews, 2000; Hilditch & McHill, Nature and Science of Sleep, 2019.

The base

A quiet graphite block: nothing to press, nothing lit while you sleep.

System

It doesn’t ring: it changes the room.

    One night

    Every change is a ramp, never a jump. The wake-up comes at the first light sleep.

    In the room

    A table lamp here; the smart bulb also fits a ceiling light.

    The same nightstand, one night: only the light changes.

    Gestures

    Four gestures replace the menus.

    01Evening

    Take the bracelet

    Means “I’m going to bed”: the wind-down starts, and the violet ring under the base dims with the room.

    02Evening

    Palm held 3 seconds

    Before you fall asleep: tomorrow off, or back on (OFF or ☀ 06:30). Forgot? In the morning hold it 5 s: today off.

    03Morning

    Palm tap

    The time is already on: the palm softens the sound but never moves the wake-up. By day, a touch shows the time for 8 s.

    04Day

    Put it back

    Means “I’m up”: everything stops, even the alarm. The night syncs; the time shows the charge, then goes off.

    05Morning

    Get up · Getting up does the rest: it is the only way to end the wake-up. The bracelet senses steps and an upright posture; there is no snooze.

    06Setup

    Pairing button · At the back, beside the USB-C: pairs the base with the phone. Pressed once, never at night.

    Light language

    One colour for each moment: the front speaks by day, the ring by night.

    Inside

    Every part inside is a catalogue part.
